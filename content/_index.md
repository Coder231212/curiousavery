      title: 'Projects built from curiosity'
      text: |
        ### LeftRightQuest

        A game-like experience for practicing left/right annotation on clinical images. The idea grows from work on vision-language model laterality and DermLatBench: turn a small but safety-critical labeling task into a repeatable quest, then learn where people and AI hesitate.

        [Explore LeftRightQuest](https://leftrightquest.org/)

        ### WoundCorder

        An AI-enabled smartphone wound assessment project that combines LiDAR-based 3D sensing and computer vision to estimate wound area and volume, with structured support for clinical documentation.

        ### Microplastics along the California coast

        A field study of mussels collected from more than 25 urban creeks, jetties, and piers between Imperial Beach and San Simeon. The project identified runoff-associated hotspots and established a baseline for future polymer and microbiome studies.

        ### Plastics and the Ballona Creek microbiome

        An independent study in the planning stage using high-throughput sequencing to characterize microorganisms associated with plastics in urban runoff and compare them with control substrates.

        ### RuKaiKai

        An LLM-powered Paiwan-English translation chatbot designed to support preservation and access for an endangered Formosan language. The work included linguistic data curation, structured prompting, and iterative evaluation.

  - block: markdown
    content:
      title: 'Why left and right matter'
      text: |
        **A chart pointed to the wrong kidney.** In a de-identified AHRQ patient-safety case, every record at one hospital named the left side even though the tumor was on the right. The patient underwent one surgery before the mismatch was discovered, then a second operation on the correct kidney. [Read the case](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/node/49726/psn-pdf).

        **A pain block went to the wrong side.** Another AHRQ case describes a patient who received a paravertebral block on the wrong side and then needed an additional block on the correct side. [Read the case](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/node/49791/psn-pdf).

        **A report can disagree with itself.** A study of more than one million radiology reports found corrected left/right discrepancies and concluded that many were clinically important. [Read the study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19380530/).

        LeftRightQuest turns this serious problem into approachable practice: make a choice, get feedback, notice the visual cues, and build a better habit for laterality annotation.
